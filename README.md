## How I work

I’m an AI-native product engineer. I use AI deeply throughout the development process as part of the systems I design, the interfaces I build, and the way I explore and ship software.

AI systems · Agent orchestration, tool calling, structured outputs, multimodal and voice interfaces, MCP, RAG, context engineering, local models, model routing, evals, and human-in-the-loop workflows.

Product engineering · I work primarily in TypeScript / JavaScript across React, Next.js, Node, Electron, Firebase, and Vercel, with Python and Java when the problem calls for them.

Applied AI tooling · I work across OpenAI, Anthropic, Gemini, open-weight/local models, speech and vision systems, and modern agent-development environments. I choose models and tooling around the constraints of the product rather than a preferred vendor.

Systems & integration · APIs, WebSockets, authentication, payments, desktop applications, local-first architecture, automation, game/mod systems, networking, and third-party service integration.

Shipping & reliability · Git/GitHub workflows, CI/CD, automated testing, browser testing, validation, observability, and designing systems that can survive continued iteration by both humans and AI agents.

I’m intentionally not tied to a single stack. I tend to learn the technology required by the product, use AI to accelerate unfamiliar territory, and rely on tests, documentation, source code, and working software to verify the result.

## Background

Before I was shipping products, I spent about a decade in IT operations and federal-sector administration — help desk and hardware at **Seatrax** and **Treasure Chest Casino**, IT & Hardware Manager at the **Gameloft** game studio, IT Specialist at the **Audubon Institute**, and the last several years across three U.S. Embassies.

As a **Consular Associate** I did biometric collection and verification, document adjudication, and fraud detection. Ten years of handling sensitive personal, biometric, and identity data under strict privacy and compliance rules is the reason I default to things like local-first architecture, explicit scope boundaries, and not storing what I don't need to store.

Studied Game and Simulation Programming at **DeVry University**.

## On working with AI

I build *with* these models, deliberately and in the open. I'd rather be judged on whether the thing works, ships, and holds up under review.

What I've found is that the interesting problem was never "can it write the function." It's orchestration: how you decompose work, where you put the guardrails, what state survives a context window, and when a human has to make the call. That's the problem I keep building tools for.
